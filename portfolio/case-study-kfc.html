KFC × OR Esports × BGMI

The KFC Loot Box

Embedding a brand into India's biggest BGMI esports tournament, not as a sponsor, but as part of the victory narrative itself.

The Challenge

KFC wanted to connect with India's rapidly growing gaming community but had never activated within the esports ecosystem. While the brand recognized gamers as a high-value consumer segment, it lacked the cultural credibility and strategy needed to engage them authentically.

The category leader had already claimed the mainstream esports space. KFC needed a different entry point—one that felt native to gaming culture, not borrowed from traditional sponsorship playbooks.

The Idea

We embedded KFC into one of India's biggest BGMI esports properties by making the brand an integral part of the fan experience rather than a traditional sponsor.

Alongside year-round visibility through a partnership with OR Esports, KFC became the Title Sponsor of Legends Rise and was positioned as the tournament's official "Winner Winner Chicken Dinner" partner—a phrase already synonymous with victory in battle royale gaming.

To drive participation and reinforce the connection, we created the KFC Loot Box, merging the excitement of in-game rewards with real-world prizes and exclusive KFC offers. The concept felt native to gaming: you earn loot in BGMI, you earn rewards through KFC.

The Execution

The campaign launched during the Legends Rise tournament with a custom 3D Colonel Sanders character dropped into the live broadcast. Viewers captured screenshots and shared them with KFC India for a chance to win exclusive KFC merchandise and in-app discounts on the KFC Loot Box meal.

To extend the campaign beyond the tournament, we launched the KFC Loot Box meal directly on the KFC app and amplified the partnership through creator-led content featuring one of India's largest gaming influencers.

Combined with OR Esports branding across the team and tournament ecosystem, the campaign created multiple touchpoints—broadcast, app, creator channels, retail—that felt native to the gaming community rather than imported.

The Outcome

By positioning KFC not as a sponsor but as part of gaming culture itself—sharing the language and mechanics of esports—the brand achieved cultural relevance with an audience that typically dismisses traditional advertising.

The campaign proved that the most authentic gaming partnerships aren't about brand logos on jerseys. They're about finding the cultural insight that makes the brand feel like it belongs in that moment. For KFC, it was "Winner Winner Chicken Dinner." For BGMI players, it was already a meme. We just gave it a real world.
